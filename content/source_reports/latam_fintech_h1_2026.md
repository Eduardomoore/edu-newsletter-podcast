## Section 03 — Trends: What the Deals Are Telling Us

Four patterns defining H1: the debt-versus-equity split, Mexico's rise, stablecoin

### Trend 01 — Structural Shift: Structured Debt Has Overtaken Equity as the Dominant Capital Form

What's happening: CloudWalk's $1.1B FIDC issuance in Q2 is larger than every single equity
round tracked across all of H1 combined — including Kavak and Plata together. Add Addi's
$150M J.P. Morgan facility, Clara's $150M Goldman Sachs renewal, 99Pay's $130M, Sol Agora's
$120M, and a dozen smaller FIDCs (Zippi, UY3, CashU, CashGO, Ume), and structured debt in H1
2026 totals well over $2.2B — nearly double the pure equity venture capital raised in the same
period.

So what: this is the clearest evidence yet that LatAm's most scaled fintechs have matured past
the "raise equity to grow" phase. They are financing loan books the way real banks do — through
securitization and receivables structures — while equity stays reserved for product,
technology, and market expansion. Cuantico VP's research flags this directly: many "megadeals"
perceived by the ecosystem are venture debt instruments that don't appear in standard VC
statistics at all. Anyone benchmarking "VC funding" without separating debt is measuring the
wrong thing.

## Section 06 — My Take: Where LatAm Fintech Is Heading

H1 2026 is the half-year that separated the fintechs building for scale from the fintechs
building for a term sheet.

Two numbers from this report tell the whole story if you only remember two. First: CloudWalk
raised $1.1 billion in a single FIDC issuance — more than every equity round tracked across
the entire first half of the year, combined. Second: Banco Plata went from $1.5B to $5B in
thirteen months, built by a team that had already done this once, in Russia, before
circumstances led them to build elsewhere. Neither of these is a "hot startup" story. Both are
stories about proven operators using proven instruments at a scale that would have been
unthinkable in LatAm fintech five years ago.

That's the real shift this half. The first wave of LatAm fintech — 2018 to 2022 — was about
proving the thesis: 650 million underbanked consumers, rising smartphone penetration, room to
build. That wave produced Nubank, Kavak, Ualá, Clip. The second wave, which H1 2026 confirms is
now fully underway, is about proving you can operate at incumbent-bank scale using instruments
incumbent banks use — securitization, sovereign wealth capital, full banking licenses — while
moving faster than any incumbent bank ever could.
