## Chapter: Introduction

Four point eight billion dollars. Ninety-plus deals. And for the first time in over a decade, Mexico just overtook Brazil in venture dollars.

This is the LatAm Fintech H1 2026 Report — covering January through June, tracking equity, debt, and M&A across Brazil, Mexico, Argentina, Colombia, Chile, and beyond.

Before we get into the numbers, one important framing note. The four-point-eight billion figure we track here covers three distinct types of capital — pure equity venture funding, structured debt and FIDC vehicles, and disclosed M&A consideration. These are not the same thing, and they shouldn't be added together into a single headline number. Standard databases like Crunchbase and FinTech Global exclude most structured debt from their VC tallies entirely. We include all three categories here because they represent real capital moving into this sector — but we'll be clear throughout about which type we're talking about. Keep that in mind as we walk through the data.

## Chapter: Four Trends Defining H1 2026

Now let's talk about what the deals are actually telling us. Four patterns stand out.

**Trend one: Structured debt has overtaken equity as the dominant capital form.**

Here's where it gets interesting. CloudWalk's one-point-one billion FIDC issuance is larger — on its own — than every single equity round tracked across all of H1 combined. Including Kavak. Including Plata. Every equity round. All of them together. Still smaller than CloudWalk's single debt instrument.

And CloudWalk isn't alone. Add Addi's one hundred and fifty million dollar J.P. Morgan facility. Clara's one hundred and fifty million Goldman Sachs renewal. 99Pay's one hundred and thirty million. Sol Agora's one hundred and twenty million. Plus a dozen smaller FIDCs from companies like Zippi, UY3, CashU, CashGO, and Ume. Total structured debt in H1 2026: well over two point two billion dollars. That's nearly double the pure equity venture capital raised in the same period.

So what does this mean in practice? It means LatAm's most scaled fintechs have matured past the "raise equity to grow" phase. They're financing loan books the way real banks do — through securitization and receivables structures. Equity is now reserved for product, technology, and market expansion. Research from Cuantico VP flags this directly: many of the so-called megadeals being celebrated across the ecosystem are actually venture debt instruments that don't appear in standard VC statistics at all. If you're benchmarking LatAm fintech by "VC funding" without separating debt from equity, you're measuring the wrong thing.

**Trend two: Mexico overtakes Brazil in venture dollars — for the first time in over a decade.**

This one is significant. Brazil has dominated LatAm venture capital for as long as anyone in this space has been tracking it. H1 2026 is the first time in more than ten years that Mexico has pulled ahead in pure equity venture dollars. Banco Plata's four hundred and five million Series C is a big part of the story — but it's not the whole story. The momentum in Mexico has been building, and H1 2026 is where it showed up in the numbers.

**Trend three: Stablecoins are moving from experiment to core infrastructure.**

Stablecoin activity in H1 was no longer fringe. Companies are building payment rails, treasury management tools, and cross-border settlement infrastructure on stablecoin foundations. This is no longer a narrative play — it's showing up in actual product architecture and actual funding rounds.

**Trend four: AI underwriting is now table stakes, not differentiation.**

If your fintech pitch deck still leads with "we use AI for underwriting" as a competitive advantage — H1 2026 is the moment that stopped working. Multiple companies across Brazil, Mexico, and Colombia are running AI-native credit decisioning at scale. The question investors are asking now isn't whether you use AI — it's whether your AI is better than the next company's, and whether you have the data to prove it.

They came to Mexico to do it again, in a market with different constraints, and they applied a playbook they'd already validated at scale.

That context changes how you read the five billion dollar valuation. This isn't a bet on a first-time team figuring it out. This is capital flowing toward proven operators executing a known playbook in a market that was ready for it.

Three years. Five billion dollars. That's the Banco Plata story.

## Chapter: Where LatAm Fintech Is Heading

H1 2026 is the half-year that separated the fintechs building for scale from the fintechs building for a term sheet.

Two numbers from this report tell the whole story. First: CloudWalk raised one point one billion dollars in a single FIDC issuance — more than every equity round tracked across the entire first half of the year, combined. Second: Banco Plata went from a one point five billion dollar valuation to five billion in thirteen months, built by a team that had already done this once before.

Neither of these is a "hot startup" story. Both are stories about proven operators using proven instruments at a scale that would have been unthinkable in LatAm fintech five years ago.

Here's the frame that matters. The first wave of LatAm fintech — roughly 2018 to 2022 — was about proving the thesis. Six hundred and fifty million underbanked consumers. Rising smartphone penetration. Room to build. That wave produced Nubank, Kavak, Ualá, Clip.

The second wave — which H1 2026 confirms is now fully underway — is about something different. It's about proving you can operate at incumbent-bank scale, using the instruments incumbent banks use: securitization, sovereign wealth capital, full banking licenses. While moving faster than any incumbent bank ever could.

That's the real shift. The thesis is proven. The question now is execution at scale. And the operators who understand that distinction are the ones raising billion-dollar debt facilities and thirteen-month five-billion-dollar valuations.

That's H1 2026.
