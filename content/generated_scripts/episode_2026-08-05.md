## Chapter: Introduction

Four point eight billion dollars. Ninety-plus deals. And a market that just changed leaders.

That's H1 2026 in LatAm fintech — and this is the report.

Before we get into the numbers, one thing you need to keep in mind. The capital tracked here comes in three distinct forms: pure equity venture capital, structured debt and FIDC vehicles, and M&A consideration. These are not the same thing. They are not directly comparable. And we are not going to add them together into one headline number, because that would be measuring the wrong thing.

Here's how they break down. Just over one point four billion in pure equity venture capital — that's a conservative estimate. More than two point two billion in structured debt and FIDC vehicles, led by one single deal from CloudWalk. And just over one point one billion in disclosed M&A consideration across twenty-three-plus transactions.

Standard data sources like Crunchbase and FinTech Global exclude most structured debt and FIDC vehicles from their VC funding tallies. We include them here because they represent real capital deployed into the sector. But read each figure in its own lane.

Now let's get into it.

## Chapter: The Deals — H1 2026 Top Transactions

Let's talk about where the money actually went.

The single largest transaction of the half was CloudWalk, out of Brazil. Payments infrastructure. One point one billion dollars — not equity, but a FIDC issuance, a structured debt vehicle. The lead was Bradesco BBI, with Itaú BBA, BTG Pactual, Safra, UBS BB, and BV all participating. The purpose: funding early receivables for InfinitePay entrepreneurial clients. This is a record-breaking issuance. And we'll come back to what it means in a moment.

The second-largest disclosed transaction was an M&A deal — TransUnion's acquisition of a majority stake in Buró de Crédito in Mexico. Around six hundred and sixty-two million dollars for a sixty-eight percent stake, purchased from selling shareholders including BBVA México, Banorte, and Santander MX. The deal was signed in January 2025 and closed on March 2nd, 2026. That makes TransUnion the largest credit bureau shareholder in Mexico.

Two very different deals. Two very different capital types. Both telling the same story: this market is operating at a different scale now.

## Chapter: Trend One — Structured Debt Has Overtaken Equity

Here's where it gets interesting.

CloudWalk's one point one billion FIDC issuance is larger than every single equity round tracked across all of H1 combined. Including Kavak. Including Plata. Every equity round. All of them. Together.

And CloudWalk isn't the only one. Add Addi's one hundred and fifty million dollar J.P. Morgan facility. Clara's one hundred and fifty million Goldman Sachs renewal. 99Pay's one hundred and thirty million. Sol Agora's one hundred and twenty million. A dozen smaller FIDCs from companies like Zippi, UY3, CashU, CashGO, and Ume. Total it up and structured debt in H1 2026 comes to well over two point two billion — nearly double the pure equity venture capital raised in the same period.

So what does this mean in practice?

It means LatAm's most scaled fintechs have matured past the "raise equity to grow" phase. They are financing loan books the way real banks do — through securitization and receivables structures. Equity is now reserved for product, technology, and market expansion.

Research from Cuantico VP flags this directly: many of the deals that feel like megadeals to the ecosystem are actually venture debt instruments that never show up in standard VC statistics at all. If you're benchmarking LatAm fintech using "VC funding" as your metric and you're not separating out debt, you are measuring the wrong thing.

## Chapter: Trend Two — Mexico Overtakes Brazil in Venture Dollars

Now, let's turn to something that hasn't happened in over a decade.

Mexico has overtaken Brazil in venture dollars. For the first time in more than ten years, Mexico led the region in equity venture capital for a half-year period. That's a structural shift, not a blip. And the Banco Plata round — which we'll dig into in a moment — is a big part of why.

## Chapter: Trend Three — Stablecoins Move to Core Infrastructure

Stablecoins are no longer an experiment. They are becoming core infrastructure for LatAm fintech.

The signal here isn't any single deal — it's the pattern. Stablecoin rails are showing up inside payments products, cross-border remittance plays, and treasury management tools. Founders are no longer pitching stablecoins as a novelty. They're building with them as a given.

## Chapter: Trend Four — AI Underwriting Is Now Table Stakes

And the fourth trend: AI underwriting.

## Chapter: Feature — Banco Plata

Every half-year report needs one company that readers will still remember six months from now. This edition, that company is Banco Plata.

Not just because of the size of the check. Because of the story behind it.

Mexico. Digital banking. April 16th, 2026. Banco Plata raised a four hundred and five million dollar Series C at a five billion dollar valuation.

The company was founded in April 2023 — just three years ago — by Neri Tollardo, Danil Anisimov, and Alexander Bro. All three were previously senior executives at Tinkoff Bank. They built a bank before. They knew exactly how to do it again.

Here are the numbers. Six hundred million dollars in annualized revenue. Three point five million active credit card customers. An eight hundred million dollar loan portfolio. Three years from founding to a five billion dollar valuation.

That is not a startup story. That is an operator story. A team with a proven playbook, applying it in a new market, moving faster than anyone who hadn't done it before could.

And it's a big reason why Mexico is sitting at the top of the regional leaderboard right now.

## Chapter: Ones to Watch

Now let's talk about small checks with big signals.

The one worth highlighting here is Rexi, out of Argentina. AI-native financial reconciliation. Pre-seed round. One point two million dollars, led by NFX and Pear VC.

At one point two million, Rexi barely shows up on a capital-deployed chart. But NFX and Pear VC are not tourist investors in LatAm. They write small pre-seed checks only when they see genuine category-definition potential.

The pitch: AI-native reconciliation for high-volume transaction businesses — automating a workflow that eats thousands of finance team hours every year. And coming out of Argentina, where financial complexity is a forcing function for exactly this kind of tooling, is a feature, not a bug.

The risk is real. Globally, this space is crowded — Ramp, Mosaic, NetSuite are all playing here. Rexi needs to prove a LatAm-specific moat. But the investors backing them suggest they believe that proof is possible.

Other early-stage companies tracked in this section — UnblockPay, Guarda, Vitrify — are worth keeping on your radar for the same reasons: small checks, early signals, specific bets on where infrastructure is going.

## Chapter: Where LatAm Fintech Is Heading

H1 2026 is the half-year that separated the fintechs building for scale from the fintechs building for a term sheet.

Two numbers from this report tell the whole story. Keep just these two.

First: CloudWalk raised one point one billion dollars in a single FIDC issuance — more than every equity round tracked across the entire first half of the year, combined.

Second: Banco Plata went from a one point five billion dollar valuation to five billion dollars in thirteen months, built by a team that had already done this once — in Russia — before circumstances led them to build elsewhere.

Neither of these is a "hot startup" story. Both are stories about proven operators using proven instruments at a scale that would have been unthinkable in LatAm fintech five years ago.

That's the real shift.

The first wave of LatAm fintech — 2018 to 2022 — was about proving the thesis. Six hundred and fifty million underbanked consumers. Rising smartphone penetration. Room to build. That wave produced Nubank, Kavak, Ualá, Clip.

The second wave — which H1 2026 confirms is now fully underway — is about proving you can operate at incumbent-bank scale, using the instruments incumbent banks use: securitization, sovereign wealth capital, full banking licenses. While moving faster than any incumbent bank ever could.

That's where this market is going. And the deals from the first half of 2026 are the clearest evidence yet that we're already there.
